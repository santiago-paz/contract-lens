## Design specificity verdict

**Design review.** The verdict is split. The product screens were made for this product:
- The analysis log uses the real step names.
- The record uses the real field names.
- The urgency chips follow the product's own rule.
- The sample data reads as convincingly German.

The frame around them is a common SaaS template. It has a centered hero over a tinted panel, three numbered step cards, a card grid, a dark band with three icon columns, four icon cards and a split contact form. Every section opens the same way: a small grey label, a serif headline, then a summary. Swap the screens, and the page could sell HR or compliance software.

The biggest missed chance is the design's own idea. DESIGN.md's North Star is "The Second Pair of Eyes": the software reads, and a person checks. No screen shows a person checking. The hero ends on the machine's "Done". The alert history is the product's most distinctive feature, and it gets one sentence and three icon columns.

**Automated scan.** The code scan found 18 minor notes and no real errors. 17 are font sizes that aren't in DESIGN.md's list of sizes. Most of them are sizes the doc already describes in its text. Three are real:

## Overall impression

The page is calm, honest and easy to read. The product screens are the best part. Three things let it down:
- The signature animation breaks for German visitors.
- The first click after the landing opens a page that looks like a different product.
- The page asks lawyers for a real contract, but it has no Impressum.

The biggest chance is to show the idea the design is built on: a person checking what the software read.

## What's working

1. **The product screens are honest.** The log uses the real steps, and the record uses the real fields. Red means "soon" everywhere. Nothing links a field to a passage, and there are no fake numbers.
2. **The system is disciplined.** There is one call to action and one accent color, with one meaning. Alternating backgrounds separate the sections without lines. Text contrast is strong: body text is 9.8:1, and the grey labels stay at 4.8:1 or more on every tint.
3. **The German version is real work.** It uses Sie, real legal terms, and German date and money formats. The page language switches with the toggle, and nothing overflows at 390px or 1440px.

## Priority issues

**[P1] The hero freezes for German visitors, and the hero text needs JavaScript to appear.**
- **Why it matters:** German firms, and reviewers checking the translation, click DE first. On a phone or a 1366x768 laptop, the page's signature element then sticks on "Schritt 0 von 2" with grey bars. The design review reproduced this in headless Chrome.
- **Cause:** `Extract.tsx:63` puts `key={language}` on the div that holds the scroll watcher. A language switch replaces that div, but `useInView` on line 34 keeps watching the old one. So the animation never starts.
- **Fix:** Move the key to the component with `<Extract key={language} />` in `Hero.tsx:59`, and drop the inner key. Also render the hero headline, lead and button visible from the server. Today they are invisible when the page arrives, and appear only after JavaScript runs.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] "Sign in" opens a different-looking product, and nothing leads to sign-up.**
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md says reviewers can create a firm and try the app, and that the app must match what the page promised. The landing's screens promise a white, plain app with sentence-case text. The login page is neon green and all capitals, with strings like "AUTHENTICATION_PROTOCOL_V2" and "INITIALIZE SESSION". It has no link to `/register`; only invitation emails reach that page.
- **Fix:**
  - Restyle `/login` and `/register` with the landing's colors and type: a white sheet, Plex, the dark pill button and the red § disc.
  - Use plain labels: Email, Password, Sign in.
  - Add "New to Contract Lens? Create a firm."
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

**[P1] The demo request asks for trust that the page hasn't earned yet.**
- **Why it matters:** The form asks lawyers, who are bound by secrecy, to bring a real contract. A German firm won't send a form from a site with no Impressum and no privacy notice. Also, the confidentiality summary says "only your firm can open it", but the next card says the text goes to outside AI providers. That breaks your first principle: claim only what exists.
- **Fix:**
  - Add Impressum and privacy links in the footer and under the submit button. These need your details, so this part waits on you.
  - Name the person who replies.
  - Say where the database runs, which is in the US.
  - Change the summary to "Only members of your firm can open it in Contract Lens."
  - Put a visible "Sample data" tag on each product screen. Today only screen readers announce it.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] The screens show the machine, never the person, and the sample list works against the product.**
- **Why it matters:** A design reviewer looks for the idea, and today it lives only in DESIGN.md. In the expiring list, 3 of the 4 contracts end before their notice period does. For example, the lift contract ends in 5 days but has 3 months' notice. A lawyer reads that as "this tool warns me too late".
- **Fix:**
  - End the hero animation on a review step: the fields checked, and a "Save record" button. Still no links to passages.
  - Replace the firm section's icon columns with an alert-history screen. Show when the alert was set, answered and closed, each with a name and a date.
  - Pick sample rows where the notice period still fits in the time left.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

**[P2] Template habits and repeated explanations make the page longer and more generic.**
- **Why it matters:** You asked for the least possible AI feel, and both reviews found the same habits. The page is also long: about 10,800px on a phone. It tells the same read, check and save story four times.
- **Fix:**
  - Remove the ruled lines from the navy blocks. They break DESIGN.md's own rule against paper looks.
  - Keep the grey label only where it adds something, instead of above all six headlines.
  - Flatten the step-one figure into one box.
  - Cut the hero lead from 57 words to about 25, and drop or shorten the figure caption.
  - In each section, merge the summary with the second paragraph.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

## Minor observations

- The headline copy has two lines, but `Hero.tsx:33` joins them. From 1024px it breaks as "Get the / facts". Render each line as its own block.
- "Sign in" wraps onto two lines in the nav at 1024px. It fits by 1100px.
- Field borders are 1.3:1 against white, but the edges of form fields need 3:1. The grey #8f949b reaches it. Placeholders are 3.1:1.
- In dark mode, `globals.css` paints the body near-black behind the landing. It can show when you scroll past the edge of the page. Give the landing's `html` a white background.
- The nav links scroll the page but never move keyboard focus. So screen-reader users stay in the nav.
- The submit button says "Send message", but every other button says "Request a demo".
- The hero lead lists service-agreement fields as if every contract has them. NDAs have no notice period and no liability cap.
- The red dot next to "Recognized as" in step one breaks "red means soon". Use the dark ink color or a check mark.
- The 13px caption under the hero runs about 108 characters per line.
- Some paragraphs end with one word alone on the last line. `text-wrap: pretty` fixes most of them.

## Questions to consider
